02

The Ratios

FormatDev : Finished HrNotes
Classroom ILT 34 – 49 : 1 The anchor benchmark (Chapman ~43:1, ATD 40–49:1). Slides, guides, handouts, facilitator prep. Use as the credible floor in any discussion.
Hands-On / Lab 50 – 100+ : 1 Not isolated in the published studies, so it's inferred. Carries everything classroom does plus lab build, equipment, task breakdowns, practical assessment, safety validation, trainer qualification.
VILT 40 – 60 : 1 Classroom effort plus platform build, virtual engagement design, and producer/facilitator rehearsal.
Self-Paced e-Learning 73 – 160+ : 1 Rises fast with interactivity. Low end is page-turner; high end is simulation and branching. Highest cost per finished hour of any format.
Read before you quote. These are consultant/contractor benchmarks. Internal staff runs roughly 60–70% of the contract cost, but the hours don't shrink. If you're defending PLL capacity, hours-per-course is the honest number, not the discounted cost. And a stakeholder hearing "80 hours to build one hour of hands-on" tends to disbelieve it on the spot, so lead with the classroom floor, then show the hands-on multiplier with its drivers itemized.
03

Why Hands-On Costs More

ADDED TO EVERY LAB BUILD

  • Lab / shop setup and station design
  • Equipment, tooling, and consumables
  • Step-level task breakdowns and job aids
  • Skills checklists and practical assessments

WHAT PUSHES IT TO THE HIGH END

  • Safety validation on live equipment
  • Trainer qualification on the actual hardware
  • New / first-of-kind equipment with no prior content
  • Heavy customization or multiple SMEs to reconcile
04

Worked Example

A new 3-day (24 finished-hour) hands-on course, moderate complexity, internal build at $65/hr loaded.

24 finished hrs × 65:1 (moderate hands-on)= 1,560 dev hrs
1,560 hrs ÷ 40 hr week≈ 39 dev-weeks
1,560 hrs × $65≈ $101,400
Planning range (50:1 – 100:1)1,200 – 2,400 hrs
How to use this: The tool gives you a defensible midpoint and a range. Bring the range to the intake conversation, not a single number, and name the drivers that put a given course toward the high end. When in doubt, estimate high and beat it.
